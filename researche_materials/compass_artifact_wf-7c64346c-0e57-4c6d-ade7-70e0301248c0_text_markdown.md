# Closing the Sharpe gap in your multi-agent crypto LLM system

**Your hybrid finding (BTC=quant, ETH=LLM, Sharpe 2.94) is the best-supported architecture in the 2024-2026 literature — keep it and formalize it rather than try to "fix" pure-LLM BTC.** The FINSABER framework (Li, Kim, Cucuringu, Ma, arXiv 2505.07078) explicitly diagnoses LLM trading agents as "too timid in uptrends and too reckless in downturns" across FinAgent, FinMem, TradingAgents, and FinCon, validating exactly the bias pattern you observe on BTC. FS-ReasoningAgent (Li et al., EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2410.12464) goes further: an ablation on BTC/ETH/SOL across bull and bear regimes shows **subjective/news reasoning dominates in bull markets, factual/on-chain reasoning dominates in bear markets** — the mechanism behind your ETH > BTC asymmetry. The literature consensus (Singhi 2025; Springer 2026; MDPI AI 7/4/138; Lefort & Benhamou 2024) is that the highest-Sharpe pattern is **LLM as regime-conditional position-sizing modulator on top of a quant signal**, not LLM as primary decider. Three changes — asset-name anonymization, regime-conditional LLM weighting via HMM/BOCPD, and FinCon-style episodic verbal memory — should plausibly close 0.5-1.0 of the Sharpe gap to your 2.69-3.10 quant baseline. Beyond that, the gap is structural: pure-LLM systems do not appear to beat well-engineered systematic baselines in OOS crypto evaluation, and your 1.52 result is right around the ceiling reported in the recent literature (e.g., arXiv 2604.26747 "From Hypotheses to Factors" reports OOS 1.55 for a constrained LLM-agent crypto factor system over 2024-2026).

The remainder of this report dives into actionable findings across your four priority areas, then consolidates them into a single ranked implementation roadmap.

## 1. Sentiment analysis: anonymize the asset, decompose fact vs subjectivity, and add deterministic derivative signals

The most important sentiment finding for your system is structural rather than data-related: **the literal token "Bitcoin" in your prompts is a bearish trigger**. Glasserman and Lin (arXiv 2309.17322) show GPT-3.5 trading P&L *improves* when company/asset names are anonymized — the LLM's general pre-trained knowledge about specific tickers acts as a "distraction effect" that introduces a negative bias for assets with FUD-heavy training corpora. Bitcoin is the canonical case (2018/2022 crashes, regulatory crackdowns, energy debates, Mt. Gox, Luna/FTX contagion), while Ethereum's training data is dominated by upgrade narratives and DeFi launches that skew neutral-to-positive. Choi et al. (arXiv 2510.07517) formalize this for multi-agent debates: TradingAgents-style bull/bear pipelines that don't anonymize messages bias the aggregator toward whichever persona aligns with the base model's prior — meaning your bear agent's points are systematically over-weighted on BTC. **Replace "Bitcoin" with "Asset X" plus technical/on-chain context for the analyst and debate stages, then re-attach the identity only at the portfolio manager stage.** This is a one-day engineering change.

The second structural finding is the **fact vs subjectivity split**. FS-ReasoningAgent (arXiv 2410.12464) is the direct architectural template for crypto: split the analyst layer into a Factual agent (on-chain stats, price action, derivatives data) and a Subjective agent (news tone, social sentiment), then have a Reflection agent re-weight them by detected regime. Their ablation explains your data: BTC during 2024-2025 was driven by ETF flows (factual/structural), so quant features dominate; ETH cycles are narrative-driven (Pectra, restaking, L2s), so subjective LLM features dominate. **Implementing this split is more important than any individual data source upgrade**, because it makes the regime-conditionality explicit rather than implicit in your per-coin choice.

For **point-in-time data sources under $50/month**, the practical stack is: CryptoPanic (free, with bull/bear community votes as a structured cross-check on your LLM output); LunarCrush Individual ($24/mo, best altcoin Galaxy Score / AltRank / sentiment coverage across 2000+ coins); CryptoQuant Advanced ($29/mo, funding rates, Coinbase Premium, Korea Premium, exchange netflows); plus the free PulseReddit dataset (arXiv 2506.03861, hourly Reddit-aligned market data Apr 2024–Mar 2025 across BTC/ETH/SOL/DOGE/BNB/PEPE) and ElKulako/cryptobert (free, runs locally) as deterministic sentiment baselines. **Critical PIT caveat**: Santiment explicitly recalculates sentiment within a 12-hour window and updates over a one-month window — *do not query historical Santiment data live during backtests*; freeze snapshots. The Tie has the cleanest PIT integrity but is institutional-priced (typically $1k+/mo).

The single highest-leverage sentiment-data discovery is **the Korean kimchi premium signal**. CryptoQuant/CryptoSlate's published 2025 backtest of zero-crossings produced +1.7% average 7-day return (67% win rate) and +6.2% average 30-day return (70% win rate) — far better than the more famous Coinbase premium (~55% win rate). This directly addresses your h=7+ horizon issue with a cheap, deterministic, look-ahead-clean signal. Add funding rate z-scores, options put-call ratio (free via Coinglass/Deribit), 25Δ risk reversal skew, and stablecoin supply ratio (BTC market cap / total stablecoin supply) to the same channel.

On the **LLM-vs-FinBERT question**, fine-tuned GPT-4 beats FinBERT, BERT, Flan-T5, and Gemma-7B on crypto sentiment classification across two MDPI 2024-2025 studies (BDCC 8(6):63; Electronics 14(6):1090) and the Springer 2025 Ray et al. paper. Kirtac & Germano (arXiv 2412.19245) report Sharpe 3.05 for an OPT-based equity long-short with LLM sentiment after 10bps costs, beating FinBERT and Loughran-McDonald — but the gain depends heavily on prompt design, not model choice. The most under-rated practical technique is **market-derived labeling** (arXiv 2502.14897): instead of using human polarity labels, label each historical crypto news item with the realized forward return sign and use those as few-shot examples or fine-tuning data. This produces +11% short-term BTC accuracy and 89.6% on 227 high-impact BTC events, and mechanically debiases against the news corpus skew.

## 2. Crypto fundamentals: tokenomics is mechanical alpha, on-chain valuation models mostly are not

The "fundamentals" agent that your TradingAgents derivative is missing should prioritize **token unlock schedules** above everything else. Tokenomist's 2023 study of 16,000+ unlock events across 40 tokens (replicated by PANews, ChainCatcher, Gate, Bitget) finds that ~90% of unlocks produce negative returns, with a -30 to +14 day window of price decay; effects are largest for VC/team unlocks at >1% of circulating supply, smaller for ecosystem/community unlocks, and smallest for tokens already >70% vested. The closest peer-reviewed analog is Field & Hanka (2001, JF) on IPO lockups (1.5-3% abnormal negative return at expiration). This is **mechanical, retail-dominated, free to compute, and rarely arbitraged in altcoins** — likely the highest-Sharpe single addition available without data spending. Implement via Tokenomist.ai and CryptoRank free tiers, with the rule: short or underweight any token where `next_unlock_pct > 1%` AND recipient is VC/team/insider, especially in the T-7 to T-1 window, with bear bias decaying after T+14.

The academic factor-zoo literature is unambiguous about which fundamentals add incremental alpha. Liu, Tsyvinski, and Wu (2022, JF) show market/size/momentum span 10 cross-sectional anomalies; the 2026 "Crypto factor zoo" (Finance Research Letters 113, 565 coins, 36 factors) finds the surviving fundamental factor is **new-address-to-price ratio**, with turnover volatility and bid-ask spreads as the dominant priced features. Bianchi & Babiak's IPCA model (CERGE-EI WP710; SSRN 3935934) confirms **liquidity, size, reversal, and downside risk dominate** — implying any LLM-generated fundamentals signal must be orthogonalized against these to add value. The single most actionable framework is Cong, Li, and Wang's (2021, RFS) user-adoption ratios — UMR, AMR, TMR, PMR — which proxy network demand directly and are computable for free from CoinMetrics Community.

**On-chain valuation models have mixed survival rates.** Stock-to-Flow is largely debunked (Morillon & Chacon 2022; MDPI JRFM 2024 17/10/443: explanatory in-sample, no OOS predictive ability). NVT Ratio has decayed since transactions moved to L2/internal exchange ledgers per Coin Metrics. MVRV and MVRV-Z retain industry-replicated cycle-extreme value but no rigorous peer-reviewed cross-sectional test (Sakkas & Urquhart 2024 include MVRV in their 27 candidates and it does not survive Harvey-Liu multiple-testing correction). **The single on-chain factor that does survive Harvey-Liu screening is the Network Distribution Factor** — supply held by addresses ≥1/10,000 of supply, i.e., whale concentration. Sakkas & Urquhart (2024, J. Int'l Financial Markets 94:102012) show a two-factor model (market + NDF) explains the cross-section.

For protocol-revenue signals, Mühlbacher et al. (arXiv 2210.16846) apply DCF and multiples to DEXs, lending protocols, and yield aggregators using Token Terminal data. The practitioner approach (Token Terminal blog, Bankless) is **cross-sectional ranking within sector**: long bottom-quintile P/F + positive 30-day fee growth, short top-quintile P/F + negative growth. **Always pair with developer-activity and TVL-trend filters because survivorship bias in DeFi is severe**. TVL by itself is irrelevant per Brigida (2025, arXiv 2506.03287) — TVL-sorted portfolios are linear functions of the market portfolio, contributing no alpha once factor-priced.

The recommended **free/low-cost fundamentals stack under $50/mo**: DeFiLlama (free, comprehensive DeFi), Artemis Lite (free, 100K Sheets calls/mo, aggregates TVL/fees/dev activity), CoinGecko Demo (free, prices/metadata), Dune free tier (PIT-clean SQL on raw blocks), Electric Capital open-dev-data (free, PIT-aware developer taxonomy), GitHub API (free, 5K req/hr), Tokenomist or CryptoRank free (vesting schedules), plus optionally Santiment Pro (~$49/mo) if you want MVRV-Z and dev_activity in one place. **Critical look-ahead caveat**: most free APIs return latest-revision values; for research-grade backtests, build synthetic PIT via Dune block-level queries with explicit `block_time <= decision_ts` filters, or pay for Glassnode's PIT metric variants (added 2025, with explicit `computed_at` timestamps).

The LLM fundamentals agent should reason **type-conditionally**: branch on token type (L1, DeFi-with-revenue, governance-only, meme, stablecoin), apply DCF/multiples only to DeFi-with-revenue tokens, use Cong-Li-Wang adoption ratios for L1s, and lean on tokenomics + supply schedules for governance/meme. Always compute forward 90-day net supply change (`inflation_emissions + scheduled_unlocks - buyback_burns`) — sustained >5% positive is a structural headwind that no amount of bullish narrative can overcome. Add Hubrich's (SSRN 3055498) **carry factor**: `-(net issuance / circulating supply)`, which is free to compute and historically robust.

## 3. Multi-agent architecture: regime-conditional hybrids, not deeper debate trees

The most counterintuitive finding for your thesis is that **deeper or more elaborate multi-agent debate does not consistently improve performance**. The ICLR 2025 blogpost "Multi-LLM-Agents Debate: Performance, Efficiency, and Scaling Challenges" evaluated five MAD frameworks across nine benchmarks and found **current MAD frameworks fail to consistently outperform simple single-agent test-time compute (e.g., self-consistency)**. Self-MoA (Li, Lin, Xia, Jin 2025, arXiv 2502.00674, Princeton) shows aggregating multiple samples from a single top model beats mixing different LLMs by +6.6% on AlpacaEval — **don't waste effort on multi-provider mixing; sample GPT-5.4-mini multiple times instead**. Optimal debate length plateaus at 2-3 rounds with 2-4 agents (Du et al. ICML 2024 used 2 rounds × 3 agents); beyond that you get token-cost explosion and occasionally accuracy degradation from context overload. **Cap your bull/bear and risk debates at 2 rounds.**

The original TradingAgents authors (TauricResearch) themselves moved past prompt-engineered debate. **Trading-R1 (arXiv 2509.11420, September 2025)** is their SFT+RL fine-tuned reasoning LLM with a three-stage easy-to-hard curriculum and volatility-adjusted decisions, replacing prompt-only TradingAgents — strong signal that the debate paradigm has hit a ceiling and the next-generation pattern is fine-tuned reasoning. TradingAgents v0.2.4 (April 2026) added structured Pydantic outputs for Research Manager, Trader, and PM, plus a five-tier rating scale (strong buy / buy / hold / sell / strong sell) — **adopt these immediately**, they're a documented attack on free-text hallucination.

The most relevant published architecture beyond TradingAgents is **FinCon (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2407.06567)**, which introduces **Conceptual Verbal Reinforcement (CVRF)**: episodic self-critique distilled into verbal "investment beliefs" that propagate selectively to the agents that need updating. Singhi's BTC-only adaptive system (UCL, arXiv 2510.08068) confirms this empirically — adding a weekly Reflect agent producing natural-language critiques for the next prompts gave **+31% return and -10% bearish loss on BTC**. This is the single highest-leverage architectural addition for your system: a weekly reflection agent that reads the past week's decisions, returns, and post-mortems, distills them into 3-5 sentence "lessons", and persists them to a beliefs store that is injected into the next-decision context.

For the **bull/bear debate specifically**, three changes are well-supported: (1) use Choi et al.'s (arXiv 2510.07517) **identity-anonymized debate** — strip persona labels before the aggregator sees the arguments; (2) add a **third Skeptic-Quant agent** that explicitly compares the LLM signal with the quant baseline (this is the missing critical voice in your pipeline); (3) give the Bear agent a stronger prior — longer context with bearish historical analogs — to counterbalance the bull-bias documented in FINSABER. Use third-person prompting at the aggregator (SYCON-Bench arXiv 2505.23840 reports 63.8% sycophancy reduction).

**Replace deterministic Portfolio Manager output with verbalized confidence + Self-MoA sampling + post-hoc calibration.** Tian et al. ("Just Ask for Calibration", arXiv 2305.14975) reduce ECE by ~50% on RLHF models versus internal logits. KalshiBench (arXiv 2512.16030) finds frontier-model ECE between 0.12 and 0.40 on prediction-market questions — meaning your current binary signals are throwing away most of the calibration information available. Have analysts output P(up) as a number, run N=5 samples at T=0.4-0.6, use std-dev as system uncertainty, and apply temperature-scaled isotonic regression on a held-out calibration period. Then convert to position weight = f(verbalized_p, ensemble_disagreement, regime). For the final PM decision, wrap in conformal prediction sets (Vishwakarma et al. 2025, JPMorgan, ICML 2025, arXiv 2501.00555) — when the prediction set contains both BUY and SELL, default to HOLD.

For **RAG**, the consensus winner in finance is hybrid retrieval (BM25 + dense) with cross-encoder reranking. Akarsu et al. (arXiv 2604.01733) benchmarked 10 strategies on 23,088 financial queries: hybrid + cross-encoder rerank wins (Recall@5 = 0.816), and **BM25 alone outperforms state-of-the-art dense retrieval** because financial terminology is verbatim. Use BGE-M3 or Voyage-finance-2 embeddings with BGE-Reranker-v2 large. **Critical: every retrieval must enforce `as_of_date < decision_date - 1 trading day`** — Look-Ahead-Bench (Benhenda 2026, arXiv 2601.13770) demonstrates this is non-negotiable; LLMs perfectly recall pre-cutoff prices and crash narratives (Lopez-Lira, Tang, Zhu 2025, arXiv 2504.14765 "The Memorization Problem"). Add **historical analog retrieval**: vectorize regime-feature tuples (returns, vol, drawdown signature) and retrieve top-K analogous past 30-day windows; include their realized outcomes as context. This directly attacks FINSABER's "bull-conservative, bear-aggressive" pathology.

For **framework choice**, stay on LangGraph. Reasons: checkpointing for nightly walk-forward backtests, per-node token traces in LangSmith, clean Pydantic integration, and it's the runtime TauricResearch chose. AutoGen (now AG2) is reportedly in maintenance mode after Microsoft pivoted to a unified Agent Framework, and CrewAI's logging is too thin for finance debugging.

The literature now has multiple papers showing **hybrid quant+LLM dominates pure-LLM and pure-quant**, validating your BTC=quant/ETH=LLM finding directly. The MDPI AI 7/4/138 (Roumeliotis et al. 2025) "Hybrid Sentiment Analysis" pipeline combines FinBERT+Gemini sentiment with SMA-50 trend confirmation + VIX regime gate over 16 years on the S&P 500: 51% annual excess return net of costs, Sharpe 1.06, Sortino 2.61. Lefort and Benhamou (SSRN 4781752, 2024) combine a financial stress indicator with GPT-4 news sentiment for risk-on/risk-off allocation; higher Sharpe and lower MDD versus stress-only. Springer's 2026 "Toward a unified agentic framework for regime-aware portfolio optimization with LLM signals" reports +0.373 Sharpe gains net of costs with LLM signals + regime probabilities + tail-risk in a logistic-blended state vector. **The optimal hybrid weight is regime-conditional** — based on FS-ReasoningAgent and FINSABER, use approximately:

| Regime | LLM weight | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Bull-trending | 0.2-0.3 | LLM is too conservative; quant trend dominates |
| Sideways / transitional | 0.6-0.8 | Sentiment-driven LLM flips losses to gains (Singhi 2025) |
| Bear | 0.4 | Factual LLM helps risk control, quant overrides |

## 4. On-chain analysis: a narrow set of signals genuinely survives, most do not

Your priority-4 instinct is correct: **most popular on-chain "indicators" do not survive rigorous out-of-sample testing**. The strongest peer-reviewed signals as of 2026 are a narrow set:

The **Network Distribution Factor / whale concentration** (Sakkas & Urquhart 2024, J. Int'l Financial Markets 94:102012) is the only on-chain factor among 13 candidates that survives Harvey-Liu (2021) multiple-testing screening. A two-factor model (market + NDF) explains the cross-section. Source: CoinMetrics, Glassnode, CryptoQuant.

**USDT exchange net inflows** are a clean intraday return predictor for BTC and ETH per Chi, Chu, Hao (arXiv 2411.06327, 2025). The asymmetry is critical and worth memorizing: USDT inflows = bullish for BTC/ETH; **ETH net inflows negatively predict ETH returns** (smart-money outflow story); BTC exchange net inflows are mostly noise for direction (only the 4-hour horizon has weak signal). This is one of the cleanest published OOS results in recent crypto literature.

**Order flow / VPIN** is arguably the single highest-Sharpe academically validated signal. Anastasopoulos et al. (J. Financial Markets 2026, doi 10.1016/j.nmar.2026.101047) report annualized Sharpe 3.68 on long-short portfolios sorted on ML-conditioned order flow across 11 currencies, with 0.78%/day alpha and break-even costs of 0.48%/day. Kitvanitphasu et al. (Research in Int'l Business & Finance 2026) show VPIN significantly predicts BTC price jumps. **Caveat**: this requires high-frequency tick data (Binance/Bybit WebSocket, free; build VPIN with López de Prado bucketing) — strictly speaking it's microstructure, not on-chain.

**Funding-rate carry** is theoretically grounded (BIS WP 1087 "Crypto Carry" 2023; Ackerer, Hugonnier, Jermann NBER WP 32936 "Perpetual Futures Pricing" forthcoming Mathematical Finance) and produces large Sharpe ratios on Binance even with high transaction costs. Cross-sectional applications across many coins outperform single-asset timing. Free via Coinglass.

**Active address growth and user adoption** are robust per Liu & Tsyvinski (2021, RFS), Bhambhwani, Delikouras & Korniotis (2023, J. Int'l Financial Markets), Rzayev/Sakkas/Urquhart (2024, EJOR forthcoming, SSRN 5057290 — innovators predict, imitators don't), and Wisniewska et al. (J. Int'l Financial Markets 2026). Compute as Z-scores; use cross-sectionally rather than as BTC timing. Source: CoinMetrics Community (free).

**What does not survive scrutiny**: Stock-to-Flow (extensively debunked — Morillon & Chacon 2022; spurious cointegration; MDPI JRFM 17/10/443 confirms in-sample only); naive MVRV thresholds (Cretarola et al. 2022 — work pre-2017, decay heavily after BTC futures launch); power-law/rainbow charts (logarithmic time scaling is irrational); Metcalfe's Law (in-sample yes, OOS no); Hash Ribbons, Puell Multiple, Mayer Multiple, Reserve Risk (industry-only, no rigorous OOS validation); TVL as alpha (Brigida 2025, arXiv 2506.03287 — irrelevant once factor-priced); MEV builder/searcher dominance as direction signal (Guo arXiv 2304.11010 proves MEV is invariant to ordering, weakening it as leading indicator); cross-chain bridge volumes as direction predictor (no peer-reviewed OOS evidence). Most arXiv papers reporting "100% directional accuracy" or hash-rate-stacking 82% accuracy claims are non-causal ML extrapolations from limited test windows; treat with extreme skepticism — Erazo Soares & Mazzeu (ResearchGate 2026, "A Comprehensive Look at Bitcoin Return Predictability") evaluate 141 predictors and find most fail to consistently beat random walk.

The recommended on-chain agent **feature vector** at ≤$30/mo: NDF whale concentration, USDT exchange net inflow (BTC, ETH), ETH exchange net inflow (negative-sign feature for ETH), active-address Z-score, hash-rate-growth Z-score, funding-rate cross-sectional rank, Coinbase premium, kimchi premium, MVRV-Z (context only, not primary signal), aSOPR (confirmation only). Source 80% from CoinMetrics Community + DefiLlama + Coinglass (all free); add Glassnode Advanced ($29/mo annual) for SOPR/MVRV-Z/LTH-STH supply at daily resolution.

**Critical PIT discipline**: every "exchange flow", "miner flow", or "whale" series depends on wallet *labels* that providers update retrospectively — discrepancies between Glassnode and CryptoQuant can be 3-7% per Bitget's 2026 review. Either snapshot data at the timestamp of decision and never re-pull historicals, or use providers (CoinMetrics) that publish methodology change-logs.

## 5. The cross-cutting lesson: regime-awareness is the binding constraint

The single most important conclusion across all four research areas is that **your identified weakness — regime detection — is also the single most important documented weakness of LLM trading agents in the published literature**. FINSABER's verbatim conclusion is: "regime-awareness and adaptive risk management are more critical than increasing architectural complexity." Mudbari's BTC LLM regime study (engrXiv/dev.to, March 2026) provides the cleanest empirical pattern: LLMs scored **0% regime-detection accuracy on the 2019-2020 institutional accumulation regime** because slow, structural, low-narrative regimes are invisible to text-driven models. CUSUM detected 5/6 historical regime transitions; LLM detected 3/6 but with **0% false-alarm rate**. The right architecture is **statistical detector as fast first-stage, LLM as second-stage interpreter** — not LLM as primary regime classifier.

The recommended regime stack: replace your SMA30 with an **HMM-3 (bull/sideways/bear) + BOCPD ensemble** trained on returns, realized vol, ADX, and funding rate, with majority voting. Add an **LLM regime-confirmer agent** that takes (HMM probabilities, recent news, on-chain summary) and outputs a confidence-weighted label plus a 1-sentence narrative explanation, used only when HMM `max_prob < 0.6`. Use Hurst exponent on rolling windows as a complementary trending vs mean-reverting filter (H > 0.55 trending; H < 0.45 mean-reverting per crypto practitioner thresholds; MDPI Mathematics 12/2911 2024 validates Hurst-anti-persistent values for crypto pairs trading). Make portfolio weights regime-conditional per the table in Section 3. Suspend LLM-recommended trades when regime entropy exceeds threshold (Springer 2026 framework).

For BTC bias measurement, monitor systematically: confusion matrix of LLM call × realized direction (rolling 30 days) with bias index = P(SELL|UP) − P(BUY|DOWN); Brier score and Brier Skill Score versus base-rate baseline (KalshiBench reports BSS < 0 — i.e., worse than always predicting base rate — is common for crypto LLMs); ECE on verbalized P(up) binned into 10 buckets; and Identity Bias Coefficient from Choi et al. (arXiv 2510.07517).

## 7. Honest caveats on the gap

A realistic assessment: closing 1.52 → 2.69-3.10 entirely with LLM improvements is unlikely. FINSABER's empirical conclusion is that LLM agent reported advantages "deteriorate significantly" under cross-sectional, longer-term evaluation; arXiv 2604.26747 (constrained LLM-agent crypto factor system) reports 1.55 Sharpe OOS over 2024-2026 — within noise of your 1.52, suggesting the regime is a binding constraint independent of any specific architecture. **Your hybrid BTC=quant/ETH=LLM=2.94 already captures most of the closeable gap**; the published literature consistently points to hybrid > pure-LLM and hybrid > pure-quant, with regime-conditional weighting as the dominant lever. The remaining wins should come from formalizing the regime conditionality, reducing drawdown via calibrated position sizing rather than chasing higher returns, and adding orthogonal deterministic signals (token unlocks, kimchi premium, NDF, USDT netflow) that the LLM cannot generate. **Items 1-7 in the roadmap are the master's-thesis sweet spot**: ~$60/mo total data cost, 3-5 weeks engineering, well-cited, and directly attacking the documented pathologies of your system class.

The thesis-defensible framing of your contribution is: **you have empirically reproduced FINSABER's diagnosis on cryptocurrency markets (a previously untested asset class), confirmed FS-ReasoningAgent's regime-conditional ablation pattern via your BTC vs ETH asymmetry, and demonstrated that the optimal architecture is regime-conditional hybrid quant+LLM with asset-specific weighting — consistent with the broader 2025-2026 consensus that LLMs in trading work best as modulators of, not replacements for, well-engineered systematic baselines.**
