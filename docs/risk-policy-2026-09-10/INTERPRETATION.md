# What the risk-policy comparison establishes

The comparison identifies a potentially useful change to stop re-entry, while daily volatility resizing mainly improves control of the specified risk measure. Neither change validates a strategy. All results use the same spent development history, qualified daily price proxies, assumed funding and threshold stop fills.

Daily resizing eliminates all observed active-date breaches of the nominal 15% risk budget:4,458 in the original control become zero. That measure is position weight times the causal volatility estimate, not realized portfolio volatility. Daily resizing improves average returns in only 7/18 configurations and leaves all 36 sleeves permanently halted by the end. It is a functioning risk control with mixed economic consequences, not a general repair that makes the old strategies profitable.

Waiting for a new saved target episode after a price stop improves average and compounded index returns in 14/18 configurations; combining waiting with daily resizing also improves 14/18. Positive primary-return configurations increase from 7/18 to 10/18. Four previously negative long/short identities become positive with either waiting policy under every registered cost assumption:90-day momentum,180-day momentum,20/100 moving-average crossover and 50/200 moving-average crossover. Cross-asset momentum instead becomes negative. These observations identify where the historical comparison changes; no champion is selected and no adoption gate passes.

The aggregate improvement needs a substantial qualification. Seven of those fourteen improved indices are long-only variants that remain negative and almost stop trading. Across all 14 long-only sleeves, either waiting arm has only 118 active dates out of 17,360, alongside 16,671 waiting dates and 571 other flat dates. Every long-only sleeve remains gross and net negative. Their avoidance of the permanent halt reflects near-total inactivity. Among the 22 remaining sleeves, only four avoid the halt. The headline reduction from 36 halted sleeves to 18 is therefore mostly an exposure-suppression effect.

The earlier favorable examples also limit the interpretation. Thirty-day long/short momentum falls from primary Sharpe 0.985 to 0.856 with daily sizing,0.769 with waiting and 0.644 with both. The 10/50 long/short moving-average case falls from 0.859 to 0.203,0.712 and 0.540 respectively. The original examples are not rescued by these controls. All 18 configurations and all sensitivities remain in the [complete results](RESULTS.md); these examples are contextual, not a new selection rule.

The mechanism is reproducible within the registered model. All 144 original control traces and 72 control return files match. Independent checks reconstruct all 576 new sleeve books, their opening and closing costs, signed funding, stop anchors, target blocks and permanent halts. All 72 primary identities,288 cost-specific indices,72 invalid-log shadows and 54 direct contrasts are retained. Convention forensics still matters: invalid log accounting changes one primary positive/nonpositive classification and changes the sign of two Sharpe differences. The simple-return results govern throughout.

The useful next research question is whether the observed long/short re-entry effects survive a separately registered prospective comparison with verified venue economics. Historical retuning cannot answer that question. The [fresh-validation plan](fresh-validation-plan.md) preserves the unresolved candidate/family, venue, funding, execution, power and gate requirements. Settlement recovery remains deferred. No paid-data purchase, new data fetch, provider contact, model fit, holdout read, paper restart or deployment occurred. **Zero strategies are validated.**
