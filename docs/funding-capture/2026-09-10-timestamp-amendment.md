# Prospective timestamp matching amendment

This data-ingestion amendment follows the three-symbol public pilot at `20260910T111531Z-6f9b0d6b`, captured on source `84f63f6`. BTCUSDT, ETHUSDT and SOLUSDT each returned 26 regular events; 14 of each symbol's event labels were a few milliseconds after an exact UTC hour. Observed offsets ranged from zero to nine milliseconds. The existing exact-hour cadence comparison would reject those labels. No strategy result, paper journal, historical replay or daily funding-coverage result was generated before this amendment.

The new snapshot reader may derive an hourly comparison label only when a raw timestamp falls in the inclusive interval from that UTC hour through one second later. This one-second bound is a conservative engineering policy for the observed timestamp jitter, not a provider guarantee or proof of economic completeness. Raw funding times, published rates, associated marks and local receipt times remain immutable. The legacy store loader and prior measurement artifacts are unchanged.

Derived labels are used for cadence comparison and identification of the following boundary event. Daily summation retains raw event membership in `[D 00:00, D+1 00:00)`. The following boundary must be a real captured event, including its actual delayed timestamp within the exhausted query. No event is moved to the preceding day, manufactured, forward-filled or assigned a zero rate. Timestamps just before an hour or more than one second after it are not aligned. Multiple distinct raw timestamps mapping to one hour are ambiguous and cannot be accepted, even when their numeric values agree.

Coverage must disclose this policy, observed offsets and any ambiguous/off-grid events. Prior seven-day cadence inference remains qualified: missing faster events can alias a slower regular grid, and current schedule observations cannot prove a past schedule. Synthetic tests must cover accepted millisecond jitter, day boundaries, the one-second limit, before-hour timestamps, duplicate mapped labels and missing events. This amendment changes operational input matching only; all gates, research windows, financial outcomes and ledger rows remain frozen.
