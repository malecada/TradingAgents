## What the 504 invented states establish

Its Cartesian grid has seven inventory ratios `u=q*S_initial/C`, three assumed premium ratios, three held hedge quantities, two jumps, two invented option requirement ratios and two invented hedge requirement ratios: 504 states. Initial allocations are 40% options, 50% hedge and 10% idle, with no transfers. Premium receipt is offset by the initial option liability, so it creates no initial equity.

For a hypothetical equal-quantity straddle with strike equal to the reference spot, the assigned liability after multiplier `J` is `u*abs(J-1)`. With premium ratio `p` and held hedge ratio `h`, normalized option equity is `0.4+u*p-u*abs(J-1)`, hedge equity is `0.5+u*h*(J-1)`, and total equity adds the idle `0.1`. Separate modeled requirements reduce available buffers; they are not cash expenses. Direct buffer tests agree with the independently rearranged inventory ceilings in every state.

Positive total equity can therefore conceal failure of the separate options wallet. At the smallest tested inventory ratio, `u=0.01`, both buffers survive every state in this finite grid. These results justify checking separate wallets and inventory sizing; they establish neither universal feasibility nor universal rejection of the policy.
