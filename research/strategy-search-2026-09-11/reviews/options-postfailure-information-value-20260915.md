## Adjudication of the single proposed two-second phase

The coordinator subsequently specified exactly one phase: hourly known
acquisition not before nominal hour+2,000 ms, selected acquisition immediately
after known processing (and initial selection), all within the original absolute
nominal hour+5,000 ms deadline. Bootstrap, daily and final capture times remain
unchanged. **This is an acceptable concrete engineering candidate under the
conditions above; it is still not an empirical grant.**

The chosen two seconds addresses the observed roughly one-second cached index
age plus at most 153 ms upper venue-offset bound, leaving a candidate timing
margin without enlarging the allowed event age. This is a mechanistic reason
for one phase, not a statistical estimate of refresh frequency or a guarantee
that an event advances when requested later. If a hypothetical refreshed event
is at T+1,000 ms with the previously observed 153 ms upper offset, its earliest
local time is T+847 ms and maximum age at action is 4,153 ms. If the cache does
not advance, the exact same old event still fails. The latter is a required
negative fixture, not grounds for an adaptive delay or another quote.

Financial chronology remains coherent only with these explicit invariants:

- The ledger's entry, rebalance and exit action timestamps stay at each nominal
  hour+5 seconds. Delayed collection changes when evidence arrives, not the cash
  transaction time. No receipt after that action may price or choose the action.
- The selected exit remains expiry minus 24 hours, with its final closing quotes
  acquired in that last hour's same two-second phase before its unchanged action.
  Final funding-history requests and their grace interval remain unchanged.
- Funding at a nominal hourly boundary remains assigned to the inventory held
  before the T+5-second action. A venue event at T, observed through a clock
  receipt near T+2 seconds, is still before the action if its entire admitted
  local interval is before it; narrowing that interval or moving the action to
  the receipt time would change cash ownership and is prohibited.
- A nextFundingTime observed near T+2 seconds must still be provably future at
  its actual controller receipt. Just-settled events may be known from earlier
  forecasts or retained finalized history; no rate or schedule is extrapolated.
  All announced/unannounced final events and conflicts retain the existing
  normalization rules. Funding bounds crossing an action stay unavailable.
- Both assets' benchmark series use the same phase and actual event/receipt
  freshness rules through the later exit. Statistical rows remain the fixed
  hourly action sequence; there is no post-hoc alignment, dropped bad hour or
  reused early-phase benchmark to improve beta availability.
- Initial rules and every hourly known/selected journal retain their original
  nominal observation window. The transport anchor may be later, so its
  monotonic deadline must be recomputed from the **remaining** time to the same
  absolute T+5-second deadline. The three seconds are a shared total for known
  HTTP, decoding, initial selection, selected-journal construction, selected HTTP
  and durable publication, not three seconds for each component.

The new module's focused proof must cover funding immediately before and after
an unchanged action, a clock interval touching it, a forecast already due at its
later receipt, and both assets with different selected exits. It must also cover
known-group completion too late to leave time for selected acquisition, initial

deadline. Such cases must retain unavailable/missed evidence or fail closed,
not shorten clock uncertainty or extend time. Verify these against the preserved
accounting engine and old failed-source claim without changing either.

The correction therefore has a bounded causal target and preserves financial
