## Correctness findings and independent checks

The initial implementation had material defects: received bodies could contradict retained partials; intent/recovery clocks were insufficiently validated; seal status/count semantics and recovery records were not checked; retained staging links could consume the member capacity needed to failure-seal; and repeated whole-inventory scans included a quadratic slot/member loop. The initial serial intent restriction also conflicted with the sixteen-request batch design.

Current source addresses these findings. Received bytes must start with the ordered retained partial prefix; interrupted recovery preserves partial members without retrying the request. Intent and recovery clocks are checked against their frozen slot meaning. Seal validation verifies exact status/count fields and forbids a complete seal with unfinished slots. Recovery records bind the claim, slots and corresponding recovery receipts. Publication reserves both byte and member capacity for staging and sealing. A full-window group of at most sixteen intents becomes available to the caller only after all those intents are durable; another window cannot begin while prior requests remain unresolved.

`check_options_journal_adversarial.py` independently exercises five disposable file-state attacks. The original intermediate result remains in `options-journal-adversarial-review.json`. The stable-source result, `options-journal-adversarial-review-v2.json`, passes all five: contradictory partials, out-of-window intent, failed-to-complete seal relabel and malformed recovery are rejected; repeated post-link interruption pressure still permits a failure seal. These are synthetic tests only.

Source inspection confirms exclusive creation, persistent invocation locking and lock-inode checks, claim hash and explicit source callback checks, fsync before durable publication, directory/parent synchronization, retained pending artifacts after interrupted publication, no overwrite/retry of closed slots and refusal to resume sealed or outer-terminal claims. Read-only opening requires existing journal/spec/lock rather than recreating them. Existing symlink components and nonregular journal members are rejected. This is not protection against a hostile process racing ancestor-directory replacement; it is a dedicated journal with cooperating writers and advisory locking.

Full validation now indexes members once. Successful locked appends update an invocation-local inventory only after publication; failed publication invalidates the cache. Entry, recovery, explicit validation and sealing reconstruct the full inventory. External modifications are detected at those boundaries, not claimed to be detected instantaneously during cached appends. No cache result is an independent authority to change the frozen claim or specification.

## Practical resource evidence and preserved failure

The attempt to simulate all 17,000 durable writes in one 120-second process failed at 120.026 seconds, retaining 8,816 intents and 8,814 receipts. `options-journal-full-construction-failure.json` preserves the outcome and qualifications. This is a failed compressed full-construction workload; it does not establish full-history construction within that bound.

The subsequent proof directly prepopulated 16,976 invented slots as a separately measured fixture, then exercised the actual journal against the near-complete history. Setup took approximately 3.01 guarded seconds and is explicitly not a production durability demonstration. Its reports remain separate from the measured invocation.

The actual invocation validated entry, durably appended a late sixteen-slot group and the final eight slots, reconstructed all records, sealed and reopened read-only. The final journal contains **17,000 slots, 34,003 members, 200,773,105 bytes and 139,264,000 raw bytes**. The guard reports exit zero, no limit reason, **37.646 seconds** and **157,376,512 bytes sampled aggregate RSS**, under 120 seconds and 512 MiB. Recorded component times are 6.184 seconds entry, 0.0867 seconds late-group publication, 0.0384 seconds final-group publication, 8.175 seconds explicit full validation, 7.368 seconds seal and 15.575 seconds read-only reopening/verification.

These measurements support the intended short-invocation architecture on the tested filesystem. **Entry validation exceeds the five-second observation window and must occur before that window.** The measured late-group publication excludes networking and actual historical admission. It cannot prove that sixteen real responses arrive within the required interval. Full source/history admission and worker-package verification need their own measured boundaries. No fsync, raw retention or cap was weakened to obtain the passing near-complete result.

## Integration requirements and disposition

The outer protocol must bind claim creation, cumulative grant, exact future envelope, selection and resolved request recipes, monotonic transport deadlines, response metadata semantics, missing-slot policy, total lifecycle resource accounting and financial evaluation. Journal request objects are frozen caller-supplied identities, not an endpoint allowlist or network authorization. A receipt's `received` state means retained bytes; economic/source admissibility still requires the independent parser and clock checks. Source sealing is an internal phase artifact and does not complete the outer claim or authorize analysis.

The new outer helper must preserve all intended cases if a journal cap, unavailable input, interruption or terminal failure occurs. Validate the actual claim and source epoch at controlled boundaries, keep per-append callbacks appropriately bounded, and preserve the original nineteen-terminal history through its separately reviewed route. Bind and test the complete selection-to-worker-to-seal-to-analysis transitions before capture.

The primitive is accepted for that integration work. Actual power-loss/hardware behavior, hostile concurrent filesystem mutation, real transport, cross-host copying, complete historical admission overhead, prospective scientific validity and profitability were not tested. No additional generic storage framework or repeated benchmark is justified by this review; the next useful work is the narrow outer admission and worker handoff contract.
