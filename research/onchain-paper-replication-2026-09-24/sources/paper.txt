Abstract
Decentralized and transparent nature of cryptocurrencies have lately increased
investors interest in them. Forecasting cryptocurrency’s price accurately is crucial
to come up with a good investment strategy, and such a forecast requires one to
consider its unique attributes as well as high volatility. Even though many existing
studies have focused on analyzing the cryptocurrency transaction graph topology,
studies on the analysis of transaction graph’s impact on prices are quite limited. In
this paper, we explore the forecasting ability of blockchain transaction graph-based
attributes on Bitcoin’s and Ethereum’s future price via deep learning methods.
More specifically, we came up with motif convolution module (MCM), a motif-
based graph representation learning approach to take local structural knowledge
into account more strongly in node and edge-attributed transaction graphs encod-
ing substantial structural knowledge. Our proposed MCM constructs a motif dic-
tionary without supervision, and employs a new motif convolution operation while
extracting the vertices local structural context. Afterwards, we learn high-level ver-
tex embeddings by using such structural context via multilayer perceptron and graph
neural network. Overall, we extract the attributed transaction graphs temporally-
evolving low-dimensional representations, and use such embedding data together
with historical prices within self-attention-based LSTM to predict the future prices
accurately. Our proposed approach outperforms all considered baselines in terms of
both price and price direction prediction, showing the promise of efficient integra-
tion of transaction data into cryptocurrency price prediction.

1 Introduction

World countries financial systems are turning into more digital systems extraordinar-
ily fast (Huawei & Oxford Economics, 2017; Patro et al., 2011; Sefer, 2022a). Cryp-
tocurrencies have been created from an open, anti-forgery registry framework called
blockchain. Such cryptocurrency breaks away from conventional trust relationships,
and it does not rely on any central authority. Since cryptocurrency is decentralized
and transparent, it introduces a possible monetary system that protects the privacy
(Herskind et al., 2020; Sefer, 2022b). Bitcoin, which can be seen as the first crypto-
currency, came out in 2009 with the Genesis Block generation. Afterwards, alterna-
tive cryptocurrencies started to appear. Among those alternative cryptocurrencies,
Ethereum has the highest popularity. Ethereum allows the transmission of money in
a decentralized way, as well as makes it possible to participate in rule-based smart
contracts.
   Volatility of price is one of the principal issues of cryptocurrencies. Bit-
coin (BTC) prices have been extremely volatile since 2013. For instance, its price
has skyrocketed by almost 1900% in 2017, while it has decreased by almost 72% in
2018. Even though Bitcoin prices are extraordinarily volatile, they can still recover
even when the market uncertainty is quite high such as during the COVID-19 pan-
demic (Mudassir et al., 2020). Volatile Bitcoin prices depend not only on market’s
supply and demand, but also on various other cryptocurrency-specific factors such
as mining cost, competing cryptocurrencies, number of transactions in the block-
chain, etc.
   Cryptocurrency evaluation can be divided into fundamental, technical, or
quantitative analysis. Technical analysis in Bitcoin trading is commonly used as a
directional bet on the market. As technical indicators fluctuate quite frequently for
cryptocurrencies, anticipating the cryptocurrency price via technical analysis is
demanding. To handle those difficulties, several studies have combined machine
learning algorithms with technical indicators to predict particularly Bitcoin price
(Huang et al., 2019). On the other hand, a number of studies have instead focused
on quantitative forecasting of Bitcoin price. Greaves and Au (2015) generates a

Bitcoin transaction graph, and forecasts Bitcoin price in an hourly interval by
using the knowledge in transaction graph via machine learning approaches. A
number of other studies predict Bitcoin price by using blockchain data, such as

   The current studies above have focused on predicting mainly Bitcoin and

other more traditional assets. The number of recent studies that use transaction

well as utilize more modern methods to model the cryptocurrency-associated vol-
atility more accurately.
   Here, we explore the forecasting ability of blockchain transaction graph-based
attributes on Bitcoin’s and Ethereum’s future prices via deep learning. More spe-
cifically, we came up with motif convolution module (MCM), a motif-based graph
representation learning approach to take local structural knowledge into account
more strongly in node and edge-attributed transaction graphs encoding substan-
tial structural knowledge. Here, we assume cryptocurrency transaction graphs will
be modeled as attributed relational graphs (ARGs). This ARG characterization
enhances normal graph representation by relating features with vertices and links
to better explain the entities and relations between them. Our proposed MCM con-
structs a motif dictionary without supervision, and employs a new motif convolu-
tion operation while extracting the vertices local structural context. Afterwards, we
learn high-level vertex embeddings by using such structural context via multilayer
perceptron and graph neural network. Overall, we extract the attributed transaction
graphs temporally-evolving low-dimensional representations, and use such embed-
ding data together with historical prices within self-attention-based LSTM to predict
the future prices accurately. Our approach outperforms all considered baselines in
terms of both price and price direction prediction, showing the promise of efficient
integration of transaction data into cryptocurrency price prediction. Our contribu-
tions can be summarized as:

• Our proposed motif convolution module-based graph representation learning
  approach combines graph neural networks to extract vertices temporally-evolv-
  ing low-dimensional representations.
• To our best knowledge, our novel approach is the first method to systematically
  use attributed transaction graphs in cryptocurrency price prediction.
• Our approach outperforms all baselines which show the importance of efficient
  integration of transaction data into cryptocurrency price prediction.

1.1 Related Work

Lately, Bitcoin dynamics have been studied in terms of Bitcoin graph’s attrib-
utes, as well as transaction cost. In this case, difficulty and hash rate have been
identified as significantly correlated with Bitcoin price (Saad & Mohaisen,
2018). By utilizing these correlations, they develop a machine learning tech-
nique to increase Bitcoin price prediction accuracy. In another study, Jang and
Lee (2018) has found core attributes of blockchain graphs to be predictive of
highly volatile Bitcoin prices in addition to the exchange rates among major cur-
rencies. Since Bitcoin and Ethereum are decentralized cryptocurrencies, their
network activity data such as transactions, are kept on blockchain. This storage
puts forward the importance of on-chain data transactions and associated met-
rics to obtain practical knowledge of the network.
   To analyze this chain transaction knowledge, we propose a method involv-
ing Graph Representation Learning and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). Earlier
network embedding approaches utilize biased random walks to conserve verti-
ces local neighborhoods (Grover & Leskovec, 2016; Perozzi et al., 2014; Tang
et al., 2015). Another line of works (Patro et al., 2012; Peng et al., 2020; Sefer &
Kingsford, 2011; Sun et al., 2019; Veličković et al., 2019) maximizes the mutual
information between global and local embeddings by training vertex encoders.
Those approaches focus on preserving the node closeness where closer verti-
ces will have similar embeddings. Nevertheless, those methods overemphasize
node closeness at global structural knowledge’s expense, so their performance
decreases when making estimations at the graph level instead of the vertex level.
As an example, earlier random walk-based approaches inspect only a restricted
set of substructures such as subtrees as graph’s representation. On the other
hand, the most similar graph representation learning-based approach to ours
is Wang et al. (2023), which also focuses on graph representation learning on
the attributed relational graphs via motifs. Other similar methods on relational
graphs such as Zhou et al. (2024) and Xu et al. (2024) have been suggested as
well.
   To pretrain GNNs, self-supervised techniques have been designed (Hu et al.,
2020; Xu et al., 2021). Semantic and structural characterizations of graphs
are learned via self-supervised training at graph, edge, and node levels which
could be further finetuned for downstream tasks. Nevertheless, those methods
could not extract substantial knowledge in graph motifs or subgraphs. Only a
few methods have recently been proposed to handle knowledge at motif level.
For instance, earlier studies Henderson et al. (2012) and Narayanan et al. (2016)
focus on encoding local structures as binary attributes, which cannot take
local structure deformations into account. There is still considerable room for
improvement to benefit from local structural knowledge and generate vertex
representations with high explainability. Efficient measurement of similarities
among input graphs and high-quality motif dictionary’s automatic generation
appear as the top challenges of motif-based techniques.

2 Motif‑Based Graph Representation Learning

Our motif-based representation learning approach’s main idea is motif convolution
module (MCM) as seen in Fig. 1A. The motif convolution module incorporates a
motif convolution layer (MCL) attached to an optional Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)
neural network. The motifs in MCL represent spatial patterns, and they could be
established via clustering subgraphs inferred over sampled graphs as seen in Fig. 1B.
Those motifs could express different spatial structures that represent distinct local
contexts. The convolution part produces local context-sensitive vertex representa-
tions that are transformation-invariant by applying all those motifs to every vertex
in the input graph. MLP part could embed these vertex representations via further
exploration of the interactions among motifs. MCM-generated vertex embeddings
model context-aware local structures, that could enhance the capability of the fol-
lowing tasks in order to grasp rich semantic knowledge. Next, we thoroughly express
motif dictionary generation, motif convolution, and integrating MCM with GNNs.

2.1 Motif Dictionary Construction

The ideal way to learn motif dictionaries is end-to-end, but such learning will
be computationally expensive as seen in distinct domains (Besta et al., 2022;
Chu & Stormo, 2022; Vincent-Cuaz et al., 2021). So, we instead used a simpler
approach to build a motif dictionary that incorporates frequently occurring pat-
terns in input ARGs. Firstly, a huge number of subgraphs, such as k-hop neigh-
borhoods, are sampled over the dataset. A k-hop neighborhood (Nikolentzos
et al., 2020) in graph theory refers to the set of nodes that can be reached from
a specific node within k hops or k edges. In other words, it includes all nodes
that are at most k edges away from the starting node. In this case, each sampled
2060                                                                              P. Celik, E. Sefer


subgraph keeps its center vertex. We ensure each sampled subgraph encloses
the local context to the fullest extent by decreasing the subgraph sampling prob-
ability by half if the subgraph’s center vertex is part of a sampled subgraph. Via
such modification, we tend to sample unexplored local contexts with a higher
probability. In this case, a single motif may represent subgraphs that are quite
alike. We achieve such representation by grouping the sampled subgraphs into
a predetermined number of clusters via hierarchical clustering with average
linkage (Johnson, 1967). Afterwards, we select an exemplary subgraph for each
cluster as a motif. When the overall subgraph set’s size is exceedingly large for
the clustering method, we randomly split the overall subgraph set into a num-
ber of smaller subsets, and infer a representative subgraph for each subset by
applying the process above. Then, we apply this process to all representative
subgraphs to extract the resulting motifs. Given each subgraph is modeled as
ARG, we require pairwise similarities to apply hierarchical clustering between
subgraphs.

Layer and local substructure of vertex u from the input graph is calculated by con-

subgraphs should be known. In the next subsection, we concisely present an NP-

duced by Gold and Rangarajan (1996) and Menke and Yang (2020). We also pro-

3 GNN + Self Attention‑Based LSTM for Price Prediction

Once graph representations are learned for different time steps, such representation
vector and historical price become input to multivariate self-attention-based LSTM
(Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997). The prediction process is summarized in Fig. 3.
LSTM is frequently utilized in sequence-based modeling tasks, such as time series
forecasting, where it can use the data arranged in a consecutive order. In this case,
LSTM works in extracting the primary attributes which directly inputs time series
data and infers significant temporal knowledge. Latent states are updated recurrently
via LSTM by integrating the previous time step’s output as an existing input while
learning the sequential nature of time series data. Moreover, LSTM uses a cell state,
which captures longer dependencies over sequences as well as prevents gradient
vanishing and explosion problems. In addition to the cell state which models the
memory behaviour, there are 3 more gates in LSTM to forget and update the flowing
knowledge.

activation function.



4 Experimental Setup

4.1 Datasets

We have obtained Bitcoin and Ethereum transaction datasets from Bitcoin’s and
Ethereum’s public blockchains respectively, by using publicly available APIs from
2016 until the end of 2024 (Motamed & Bahrak, 2019). This period is quite com-
prehensive as it includes multiple bull and bear market periods as well as the recent
COVID-19 pandemic period. Even though the blockchain structures for Bitcoin and
Ethereum are quite different, the transaction knowledge is kept inside the blocks in
both of them. A predetermined number of transactions could be contained inside
each block.
   Generally, we can divide blockchains into 2 different types such as account-based
and UTXO (Unspent Transaction Output)-based. Bitcoin uses a UTXO-based block-
chain, where every individual transaction input is connected to a previous transactions
output. To put it in another way, the existing transactions will utilize the previous trans-
actions outputs and will generate new outputs to be utilized in the following transac-
tions. However, Ethereum is an account-based blockchain, where we explicitly state the
addresses of the incoming and outgoing accounts explicitly. In Bitcoin, every observed
transaction includes a number of inputs and outputs, whereas Ethereum’s each transac-
tion includes a single input and output. In our experiments, over a transaction graph,
vertices correspond to account addresses, and edges correspond to the transactions
between those accounts. Since blockchains each recorded block owns a timestamp, we
have partitioned our timeline into weekly intervals and generated a transaction graph
each week which includes the corresponding block’s transactions. We also obtain the
daily Bitcoin and Ethereum prices from Yahoo Finance from 2016 until the end of

2024. We run our experiments on Google Colab with Nvidia P100 with 52 GB mem-
ory. On average, Bitcoin graphs density is 0.002 whereas it is 0.003 for Ethereum.

characteristic functions, while providing vector embeddings that could be treated as
input to a machine learning algorithm. Graph Isomorphism Networks (GINs) Xu

6 Conclusions

The authors came up with a new technique which combines Graph Neural Net-
works with LSTM to combine historical prices with blockchain transaction graph-
based attributes to forecast Bitcoin’s and Ethereum’s future prices via deep learning
methods. More specifically, we came up with Motif Convolution Module (MCM), a
motif-based graph representation learning approach to take local structural knowl-
edge into account more strongly in node and edge-attributed transaction graphs
encoding substantial structural knowledge. Our proposed MCM constructs a motif
dictionary without supervision and employs a new motif convolution operation
while extracting the vertices local structural context. Afterwards, we learn high-
level vertex embeddings by using such structural context via multilayer perceptron
and graph neural network. Overall, we extract the attributed transaction graphs
temporally-evolving low-dimensional representations, and use such embedding data
together with historical prices within LSTM to predict future prices accurately. Our
proposed approach outperforms all considered baselines in terms of both price and
price direction prediction, showing the promise of efficient integration of transac-
tion data into cryptocurrency price prediction. Rigorous experiments also verify the
robustness of our approach.
   In future work, one viable study could be predicting the crypto asset prices and
dynamics via a more comprehensive framework. More specifically, a comprehen-
sive framework that combines different types of datasets such as social media data,
Google Trends, and on-chain data could be developed.
