Close immediately rejects the already-held nonblocking lock and masks the original admission/evidence failure. Use an internal close/failure path whose caller already holds the lock, preserve the primary error, and always release the outer lock. 3. **AOO3 — callbacks can invalidate a live operation after its precheck.** `_current` checks ledger terminal flags before external owner/selection callbacks, and `_live` checks operation active/terminal membership only before `_current`. Public `Operation.lease` is not transition-serialized. A callback can invoke public ledger.close after the original owner lease, creating consistent closed/poisoned ledger state and failed operation metadata. Subsequent owner/evidence checks can accept those bytes, and the original operation lease returns successfully despite closure. Recheck ledger terminal/binding and exact operation active/nonterminal state callback-free after callbacks. Serialize the public lease against close/complete when promising noninterleaved transitions. Test actual public close during a lease, and final claim/completion revocation; do not rely solely on changed-file prechecks.

4. **AOO4 — new descriptor cleanup uncertainty remains an ordinary error.** Newly owned directory descriptors use raw os.close at lines55,60,80,91,120,159 and163. A close error can mask the primary operation failure or propagate ordinary OSError despite uncertain descriptor ownership. Apply shared fatal one-shot cleanup with primary-cause preservation at these new boundaries, attempt independent cleanup and guarantee transition release. Constructor and completion/evidence close fault tests are relevant; an injected error after a real close proves propagation, not recovery of a genuinely leaked descriptor.
