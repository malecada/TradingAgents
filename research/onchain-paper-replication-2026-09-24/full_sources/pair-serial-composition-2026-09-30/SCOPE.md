# Serial pair/journal/workload composition candidate

`serial.py` connects the accepted pair journal and extent checker to the
maintained numerical PairSession. Each directional purpose is bound to exact
ordered typed graphs, numerical context/configuration and one workload identity.
The policy-derived checkpoint envelope is reserved durably before session or
numerical allocation. Each completed save undergoes extent checks before its
journal publication. Pair work is serial and mappings close before return.

Only the exact journal-published reference is reusable. Completed scores are
read without creating/resuming a solver or adding another reservation. Published
progress resumes into a fresh owner session, retaining parent references and
cumulative reservations. A bounded slice stop preserves a progress publication;
the caller must close its owner before an admitted successor. Unpublished
reservations—including fully saved orphan completions—are refused pending
explicit reconciliation, never silently rerun or adopted.

Cleanup failures are fatal BaseException subclasses, preventing ordinary
RuntimeError/OSError unavailable-cell handlers from continuing the worker.
The adapter's existing cleanup-error note is also elevated when no handle was
returned. Original code/tests and green01 are preserved; red02 demonstrates the
formerly nonfatal constructor-cleanup note. green03 reports10tests passing in
0.628seconds using tiny real PairSession arrays and journals. Tests compare
scalar scores, full dictionary matrices/identity and MCM values, demonstrate
published progress continuation and completed-score reuse, preserve ancestors,
reject pending orphans, and exercise cumulative logical quota and fatal cleanup.

This is an isolated composition component, not empirical admission. The lease
callback in tests is a sequencing hook; these tests do not exercise a real
ResearchRun, current guard, registered failed-parent death or policy admission.
The dictionary/MCM test uses their actual purpose-producing loops, but the
component itself cannot prove that an arbitrary external caller belongs to
those loops. Exact registered owner/workload construction remains necessary.
