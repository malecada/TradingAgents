## ST1 — seal drops the tail contract during destination publication

`tradingagents/research/onchain_replication/score_tail.py:237–239` checks the explicit tail lease, checks the destination, then directly returns `batches.append(...)`. The destination writer has its own independent lease; the API neither equates it with the tail lease nor carries tail verification into it. A tail lease revoked, or tail content/root changed, during destination publication can therefore leave `seal` returning success while its prerequisite is no longer valid. ScoreBatches' own post-write checks cover the batch writer and newly written chunk, not this separate prerequisite.

Required correction: complete a final combined tail lease/root/content validation after destination append and before returning success, against the originally admitted terminal hash and exact destination/score bytes. Keep rereads/allocation bounded. If that final check fails, retain both the already published destination chunk and tail, refuse successful seal, and do not silently retry or overwrite either identity. Add a regression with a valid independent batch lease while the tail lease is revoked during chunk publication, and a late retained-tail mutation case. This finding is inferred from the actual call sequence; the reviewer did not execute an additional test.

## Supported behavior and precise limits

The start manifest binds MCM kind, caller-supplied scope/owner, start ordinal/count, fixed little-endian record format and destination hash. The destination incorporates the actual ScoreBatches directory/start hash/index/offset/count. Each 80-byte record contains an unsigned ordinal, exact float64 score, 32-byte purpose hash and chained checksum. Append accepts only the next ordinal and finite [0,1] values, including zero; it loops over short writes, fsyncs, rechecks lease/identity/extent and reads back the exact record before advancing acknowledged state and returning the callback-shaped reply. A failed append poisons further appends.

Failed terminal evidence distinguishes acknowledged records from at most one pending record's bytes; a fully written but unacknowledged record remains pending rather than becoming a completed score. Two bounded verification passes compare content and signatures after the last external lease. Scope/owner, counts, hashes, finite values, record order and exact three-file inventory are checked. The procedure remains non-atomic after each entry's last read. No arbitrary source rehash is substituted for the independently supplied terminal hash.

The saved check01 log reports **12 passes in 0.22 seconds**. Coverage includes actual fsync-before-reply, signed-zero/float64 preservation and successful sealing, failed prefixes, 13-byte partial writes, lease loss after record fsync, corruption/order/owner/scope/extra/symlink refusal, invalid values/capacity and failed-tail seal refusal. It does not exercise ST1, destination-scope conflict variants, actual registered leases, power-loss recovery, real matching or process resource enforcement. The red missing-module evidence remains retained; no closed checks were rerun.

The 8 MiB bound applies to the record file, not total live memory or total retained output. Decoding additionally allocates eight score bytes and 32 purpose bytes per acknowledged cell; raw bytes and verification rereads also coexist. A complete retained tail costs 80 bytes per score plus two metadata files, while its destination adds another eight bytes per score and batch metadata. Maximum cells per tail are therefore `floor(8 MiB / 80)`, smaller than ScoreBatches' maximum float64 chunk count; callers must choose a compatible chunk count before allocating or doing matching work.

Crash-retained records without a terminal are preserved but are not accepted by the current `verify` API. Existence of a record alone cannot prove whether its callback returned before interruption. No automatic reopen, reconciliation or successor use is implemented. The tail does not itself prove that supplied purpose hashes came from the actual MCM loop, link convergence/iteration evidence, eliminate per-pair scratch/journals, or bind a persisted tail-to-batch provenance reference in ScoreBatches' header. These remain integration obligations rather than properties inferred from this primitive.
